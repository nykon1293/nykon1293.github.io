Designed and built an AI production system that knows when to stop.

I designed and built an end-to-end system that turns research into publication-ready short-form content while keeping humans in control of the words, frames, and final release. Automation handles the production work; explicit gates, verification, and fail-closed rules govern what can advance.

The problem

Scaling output was easy. Scaling judgment was not.

A private ecommerce portfolio needed a repeatable way to produce native-feeling short-form content across multiple brands. A chain of disconnected AI tools could create more output, but it could not guarantee that approved words stayed unchanged, visuals remained usable, costs were controlled, or publishing stayed under human authority.

The real design challenge was not “make a video with AI.” It was to create one governed production system where research, generation, review, verification, drafting, publishing, and learning worked as a controlled whole.

The architecture

Automation between decisions—not instead of decisions.

The production line below groups the private implementation into a safe public view. Gold stages require an explicit human decision. Green stages run automatically. A failed check cannot quietly advance.

Automated stage Human gate Feedback loop
  1. 01Research

    Study proven formats and identify the mechanics that earned attention.

  2. 02Concepts

    Rebuild useful patterns for the current brand, audience, and product constraints.

  3. 03Approve words

    A human approves the script. Approved language becomes the source of truth.

  4. 04Approve frames

    Review inexpensive storyboard stills before committing to full generation.

  5. 05Generate

    Create production assets from the locked script and approved visual plan.

  6. 06Verify

    Compare audio to approved words and inspect visual output for known defects.

  7. 07Approve draft

    The finished draft waits for a human yes. The system cannot approve itself.

  8. 08Publish & learn

    Approved work publishes; results and rejections improve the next cycle.

Design principles

The guardrails are part of the product.

The system was designed around practical operating rules, not a promise that a model would always behave correctly.

01

Make it cheap to say no

Review scripts and low-cost stills before expensive production work begins.

02

Lock what was approved

Downstream steps cannot silently rewrite the words or visual plan a person accepted.

03

Fail closed

If required verification cannot pass, the run stops and reports instead of guessing.

04

Reserve authority for people

The system can prepare a draft, but it cannot publish or mark its own work approved.

05

Turn misses into rules

Rejections and observed defects become reusable constraints for future production.

What I built

One operating system, not a pile of AI tools.

I owned the product and systems work needed to make the pipeline coherent from the operator’s point of view.

Workflow and product design

Mapped the production lifecycle, decision rights, approval states, failure states, and operator experience.

AI orchestration

Connected research, structured generation, production tasks, verification, and controlled retries into one repeatable flow.

Quality and governance

Designed source-of-truth rules, transcript comparison, visual inspection, budget boundaries, and human-only release authority.

Learning loop

Connected rejection lessons and performance signals back to the rules and concepts that shape future work.
