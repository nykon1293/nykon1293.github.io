The problem

The numbers that matter live in five different systems. Checking them takes a morning. So it does not happen regularly. Problems show up late.

What SI does

SI collects a short, fixed set of measures each week. Examples are sales, new inquiries, and jobs completed. Also cash in the bank, overdue invoices, and open complaints. It lays them next to last week and the same week last year. It adds a few plain sentences on what moved and why, where the data shows it.

Related in this library: Weekly money-in digest of payments and what is owed.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Multi-location auto repair shop

    The page shows car count, average repair order, and comebacks by location. A note says one shop's car count dropped. That was the week a technician was out.

  • Accounting firm

    Partners see returns in progress each Monday in tax season. They also see returns waiting on clients, billed hours, and unpaid invoices.

  • Food truck business

    Sales by location and day. Food cost from supplier bills. Weather notes on slow days.

What it takes to set up

Read access to the tools involved. An agreed list of no more than about ten measures. Setup is often about a week.

What a human still checks

  • The owner chooses the measures. The owner checks the first few reports against the source systems.
  • Notes are marked as likely causes, not certainties.
  • Access is read-only.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Every [Monday], pull these measures [list] from [tools] for last week. Show each against the prior week and the same week last year. Write [3-5] plain sentences on what changed and the likely reasons visible in the data, marked as likely. Keep it to one page. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

The owner sees the business at a glance every week. Changes get noticed while they are still small.
