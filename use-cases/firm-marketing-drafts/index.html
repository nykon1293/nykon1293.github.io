Marketing drafts, newsletters, and review requests

SI (Super Intelligence, the new name for AI) drafts newsletters, site updates, and review requests from topics the firm chooses. A person approves every piece before it is published.

Office help for solo attorneys and small firms. SI drafts and organizes. Attorneys review and decide. No legal advice. No filing-ready work. Client confidentiality comes first.

Worth knowing: This is office support only. It is not legal advice. It does not replace an attorney's judgment. An attorney reviews and approves anything that faces a client or is substantive. Client-confidential information is used only with the firm's agreements, access controls, and consent. Advertising rules vary by state. Ads must be truthful and not misleading. SI never writes client results. SI never writes testimonials or comparisons. SI never promises outcomes. SI never includes a client's identity or matter details. Asking satisfied clients for reviews is generally allowed. Paying for reviews is generally not. Check your state's rules.

The problem

Firms know they should send newsletters, update the website, and ask clients for reviews. The drafting rarely gets done.

What SI does

SI drafts newsletter issues, website updates, and social posts. It uses topics the firm chooses and public information such as firm news. It builds a simple calendar for them. It also drafts polite review-request messages. The firm sends those to clients the attorney chooses.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Two-attorney estate-planning firm

    A newsletter draft covers a firm announcement the attorneys chose. It has no client stories. It waits for approval.

  • Solo family-law practice

    A review-request draft is prepared only for people the attorney has already listed. A person checks that it names no matter.

  • Small commercial-litigation firm

    Website drafts use public firm news only. The calendar shows when each piece is ready for attorney review.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the firm connects anything that holds client information, it reviews the vendor's terms. It checks data retention and training practices. It confirms access controls. It gets any required client consent. Start with sample or de-identified material. Start with topics the firm chooses. Use a few past newsletters as a tone sample. Use review-request wording an attorney has approved. Setup is often a few days.

What a human still checks

  • An attorney reviews and approves every piece before it is published. The attorney picks who is asked for a review.
  • A person checks that nothing reveals client information.
  • SI never sends, files, or posts on its own.
  • Unsure items go to a "needs attorney review" queue.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Draft a newsletter, a short website update, and one social post from these topics and this public firm news only [paste]. Build a simple calendar for them. Draft a polite review request from this approved wording [paste]. Do not include any client's identity, matter details, testimonials, comparisons, or promises. Pause and show me before sending, filing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include client-confidential details.

Typical effect

Drafts and a simple calendar are ready for an attorney to edit. Nothing is published until it is approved.
