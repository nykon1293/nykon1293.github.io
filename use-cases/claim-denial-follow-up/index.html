Worth knowing: This is office support only. SI does not name a condition. SI does not recommend treatment. SI does not make clinical decisions. SI does not replace a clinician's judgment. The problem

Denied and unpaid claims pile up because nobody has time to work them in order. Appeal deadlines slip.

What SI does

SI sorts the denial list by payer, reason code, and date. It tracks each item's status and deadline. It groups routine fixes, such as missing information or demographic errors, for staff to correct. It drafts follow-up notes for the biller to review.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Small family-medicine practice

    Denials are grouped by payer and reason code, with the appeal date shown. The biller reviews the draft note before anything is resubmitted.

  • Specialty clinic

    Missing-information denials are listed separately from items that need a clinician. Staff correct only what the record already supports.

  • Independent chiropractic clinic

    Each open denial shows its deadline. Nothing is changed in the billing system until a person approves it.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the practice connects anything that holds patient information, it confirms the tool is covered by a signed BAA. It sets role-based access. It limits SI to the minimum necessary. Start with sample or de-identified material. Start from a denial export the biller provides. SI organizes the list. A trained biller still corrects and resubmits.

What a human still checks

  • A staff member or clinician approves before anything goes to a patient, a payer, or any outside party.
  • SI never sends, submits, bills, or posts on its own.
  • Anything unusual goes to a "needs clinician or manager review" queue.
  • The practice decides what patient data SI may see. Testing starts with synthetic or de-identified samples.
  • A trained biller or coder reviews each denial against the record. That person corrects and resubmits. A clinician reviews any clinical content.
