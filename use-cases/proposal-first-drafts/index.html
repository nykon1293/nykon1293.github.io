The problem

Proposals take hours to write. They often start from a copy of an old one. Leftover details from another client remain. The delay means the prospect waits while interest cools.

What SI does

SI reads the discovery notes and a few of your past proposals. Then it drafts a proposal in your usual structure. It states the client's situation in their words. It covers scope and what is not included. It covers timeline. It covers pricing options from your rate sheet. It covers next steps. It highlights any section where it lacked information. It does not guess.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Kitchen and bath remodeler

    Notes from a walkthrough become a proposal. It covers demolition, plumbing, and tile scope. It includes an allowance line for fixtures. It flags that cabinet sizes are still unknown.

  • Web design studio

    A discovery call becomes a proposal with three package options. The timeline is built from the studio's standard phases.

  • Employee benefits consultant

    Notes from a meeting with a 40-person company become a proposal outline. It includes the review steps. One section is marked for the consultant to fill in.

What it takes to set up

Your proposal template. A rate sheet. Two or three good past proposals. Setup is often a day or two.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Using these discovery notes [paste], my template [file], rate sheet [file] and these past proposals [files], draft a proposal for [client]. Include situation, scope, exclusions, timeline, [2-3] pricing options and next steps. Mark any section where information is missing. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

Proposals go out sooner. They start from a clean, client-specific draft instead of a recycled one.
