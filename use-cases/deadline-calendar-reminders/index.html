The problem

Dates are scattered across emails, notices, and calendars. A missed date is among the most serious errors a firm can make.

What SI does

SI collects dates that attorneys or staff have already identified into one calendar or tracker. It sends reminders ahead of each date. It flags dates that conflict with other commitments. It produces a weekly coming-up list per attorney. A tax firm can keep the same tracker for dates a person already verified. SI does not calculate a due date.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Small commercial-litigation firm

    Dates a paralegal has already confirmed are collected into one tracker. The weekly list shows two that fall on the same morning.

  • Solo family-law practice

    A hearing date the attorney wrote down is added to the calendar with a reminder. The source note stays attached so a second person can spot-check it.

  • Two-attorney estate-planning firm

    Each attorney gets a weekly coming-up list. It is drawn only from dates someone at the firm has already verified.

  • Two-partner accounting firm

    Dates a professional verified sit in one tracker. The weekly list shows what is coming up and what is overdue.

  • Small tax-preparation office

    Reminders go to the assigned staff member at times the firm set. SI does not add dates of its own.

  • Solo CPA

    The firm's calendar of record stays with the professional. SI only tracks what was entered.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the firm connects anything that holds client information, it reviews the vendor's terms. It checks data retention and training practices. It confirms access controls. It gets any required client consent. Start with sample or de-identified material. Use the calendar or tracker the firm already keeps. Use a written rule that only dates a person has verified may be entered. Setup is often a few days.

What a human still checks

  • An attorney or designated staff member determines and confirms each date and how it was calculated. They use the firm's docketing process and the applicable rules.
  • A second person spot-checks the list.
  • SI never sends, files, or posts on its own.
  • Unsure items go to a "needs attorney review" queue.
  • The CPA, EA, or preparer reviews and approves before anything goes to a client, agency, or third party.
  • SI never sends, files, e-signs, or posts on its own.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Collect only these dates that a person has already verified [paste the list, with who verified each one] into one tracker. Note conflicts with these other commitments [paste]. Draft a weekly coming-up list per attorney. Do not calculate or add any date that is not on the verified list. Pause and show me before sending, filing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include client-confidential details.

Typical effect

Verified dates sit in one list with reminders. Dates that collide are easier to see before the week starts.
