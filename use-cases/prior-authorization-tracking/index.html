The problem

Prior authorizations live in emails, portals, and sticky notes. A lapsed or missing authorization can delay a visit. It can also leave the practice with an unpaid claim.

What SI does

SI keeps a tracker of each request. It records patient reference, service, payer, date submitted, status, expiration, and follow-up due. It drafts routine status-check notes from approved wording. It shows what is pending, expiring, or overdue.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Specialty clinic

    The tracker lists requests by status and expiration. A draft status-check note uses the clinic's approved wording. Staff send it.

  • Small family-medicine practice

    Items due for follow-up this week are listed with the payer and the date submitted. Clinical letters stay with the clinician.

  • Solo therapy practice

    An authorization nearing its end date is flagged for staff. SI does not decide whether another visit is appropriate.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the practice connects anything that holds patient information, it confirms the tool is covered by a signed BAA. It sets role-based access. It limits SI to the minimum necessary. Start with sample or de-identified material. Use the tracker the practice already keeps. A clinician supplies any clinical information. SI does not write it.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
From this authorization log [paste], list what is pending, expiring, or overdue. For each overdue item, draft a status-check note from this approved wording [paste]. Do not write clinical justification, do not invent documents, and do not decide whether a service is appropriate. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, billing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include any patient names or health information.

Typical effect

Pending, expiring, and overdue requests are visible in one list. A person still submits, follows up, and handles denials.
