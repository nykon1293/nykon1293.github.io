Meeting and call summaries for attorney review

From notes or a transcript the attorney provides, SI (Super Intelligence, the new name for AI) drafts a summary. The summary has action items and owners. It is labeled as a draft for attorney review.

Office help for solo attorneys and small firms. SI drafts and organizes. Attorneys review and decide. No legal advice. No filing-ready work. Client confidentiality comes first.

Worth knowing: This is office support only. It is not legal advice. It does not replace an attorney's judgment. An attorney reviews and approves anything that faces a client or is substantive. Client-confidential information is used only with the firm's agreements, access controls, and consent. Recording or transcribing a call may require consent. State rules differ. Get consent first. Attorney-client conversations are confidential and privileged. Use a tool only after the firm has checked its terms, retention, and training practices. Get the client's consent where the rules require it. ABA Formal Opinion 512 says boilerplate in an engagement letter is not enough for self-learning tools. Summaries are drafts. They are not a record of legal advice.

The problem

Notes from client calls and internal meetings are thin or never written. Action items get lost.

What SI does

From a transcript or notes the attorney provides, SI drafts a summary. The summary lists action items, open questions, and who owns each. It is labeled "DRAFT, for attorney review."

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Two-attorney estate-planning firm

    Notes from an internal meeting become a draft summary with action items and owners. It is labeled for attorney review.

  • Solo family-law practice

    Notes the attorney provided are turned into open questions and next steps. The attorney decides whether anything goes to the client.

  • Small commercial-litigation firm

    The draft is marked "DRAFT, for attorney review." It is not added to the file until the attorney corrects it.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the firm connects anything that holds client information, it reviews the vendor's terms. It checks data retention and training practices. It confirms access controls. It gets any required client consent. Start with sample or de-identified material. The attorney chooses the transcript or notes to provide. Recording or transcribing a call may require consent. State rules differ. The firm gets that consent first. Setup is often a day or two once the tool has been vetted.

What a human still checks

  • The attorney checks the summary against what actually happened. The attorney corrects it. The attorney decides what goes in the file or to the client.
  • An attorney reviews and approves before anything leaves the firm.
  • SI never sends, files, or posts on its own.
  • The firm decides what client data SI may see. Testing starts with non-confidential or de-identified samples.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
From these notes or this transcript that I am providing [paste], draft a summary with action items, open questions, and an owner for each. Label the top "DRAFT, for attorney review." Do not add facts that are not in the notes. Pause and show me before sending, filing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include client-confidential details.

Typical effect

Action items are written down while the conversation is still fresh. They sit in a draft the attorney can correct.
