The problem

Many incoming messages are some version of 'where is my thing?' Answering each one means opening another system. Then you find the record and write the same kind of message again.

What SI does

SI spots status questions. It finds the matching record by name, number, address, or email. It reads the current state. That can be shipped with a tracking number. It can be a technician scheduled. It can be a part on order. It can be waiting on the customer. It drafts a reply in your tone with the real details. It flags cases where the record looks stuck. It also flags cases that contradict what the customer was told. It can also watch for a package that has not moved. The draft uses only what the record says.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Appliance repair shop

    A customer emails asking about their dishwasher. SI finds the ticket. The part is on backorder with an expected date. SI drafts a reply with that date and an offer to call.

  • Custom cabinet maker

    A homeowner asks when installation will happen. SI checks the production board. The doors are in finishing. SI drafts a reply with the current stage and the scheduled install week.

  • Online store

    A 'where is my order' email is matched to the order. Tracking shows it stalled for five days. The draft apologizes and offers a replacement option. The owner still must approve it.

  • Shopify store using a shipping app

    Two packages show a delivery exception and one has no scan in five days. The list and draft notes wait for the owner.

  • Beauty brand shipping two-day

    A failed delivery marked 'need new address' is listed with the order. A draft asks the customer to confirm the door code.

  • Outdoor gear shop

    Weekend scans lag, so SI still lists exceptions. It flags ones that already have a customer email so you do not double-reply.

What it takes to set up

Give read access to the system that holds status. That can be an order system, a job tool, a repair log, or a shared sheet. Also give access to the inbox or helpdesk. Share a few example replies. Write the rules for when a delay should go to a person. Setup is often several days.

What a human still checks

  • A person approves each reply until the drafts are right most of the time. They can keep approving after that.
  • Anything that looks stuck or overdue goes to a person. The record is attached.
  • SI does not promise dates that are not in the record.
  • A person decides whether to contact the customer, the carrier, or wait.
  • SI does not promise a delivery date that is not on the scan.
  • Address changes and claims are made by a person.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
For each message in [inbox/queue] asking about the status of [an order/job/repair], find the matching record in [system or file] using [order number, name, email or address]. Draft a reply that states the current status and next step using only what is in the record. Flag any record with no update in [5] days. Use this tone: [example reply]. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

Routine status questions get faster, more accurate answers. Stuck jobs or orders come to light sooner.
