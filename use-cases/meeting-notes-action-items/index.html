Meeting notes into owners and due dates

SI turns a meeting transcript or rough notes into a short summary, decisions and a task list with owners and due dates.

Worth knowing: Recording meetings: consent rules vary by place; tell participants and follow the stricter rule if unsure. Formal board minutes should be reviewed by the secretary.

The problem

Meetings end with agreement, then nothing happens because nobody wrote down who would do what. The next meeting starts by rediscussing the last one.

What SI does

From a transcript or notes, SI writes a short summary, decisions made, open questions and action items with an owner and due date for each. It can post the summary to the team channel and add tasks to the project tool once approved, and starts the next agenda with any items still open.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are illustrations, not client results.

  • Nonprofit board

    Board meeting notes become draft minutes, a list of motions and votes, and action items for committee chairs.

  • General contractor

    The weekly site meeting with subcontractors produces a list of who is fixing which punch-list item by when.

  • Accounting firm

    The partner meeting's decisions about client assignments are logged and tasks created.

What it takes to set up

Access to transcripts or notes, the team channel and the task tool. Setup is often a day.

What a human still checks

  • The meeting organizer reviews the summary before it is shared.
  • Task owners confirm their tasks.
  • Recording is used only with participants' consent.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool of choice and swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Here is the transcript or notes from [meeting]: [paste]. Write a short summary, decisions, open questions and action items with owner and due date. Draft a post for [channel] and the tasks for [tool]. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

Decisions stick, and follow-through is easier to track.
