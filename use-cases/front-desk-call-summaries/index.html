The problem

Messages taken at the front desk are thin. They get passed along late. Action items disappear.

What SI does

From notes or an approved call transcript, SI drafts a short summary. The summary lists caller, request, who owns the follow-up, and due date. It uses the practice's message format. It is labeled "DRAFT, for staff review." SI routes it to the right person.

Related in this library: After-hours call summary for the owner.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Small family-medicine practice

    A front-desk note becomes a draft with the caller, the request, and who should call back. Staff correct it before it is filed.

  • Two-dentist practice

    An approved transcript is turned into the practice's message format and labeled as a draft. The person who took the call checks it.

  • Solo therapy practice

    The draft names the follow-up owner and a due date. It is not filed as a clinical record until a person approves it.

What a human still checks

  • The staff member checks the summary against the call and corrects it. That person files it where the practice keeps messages. A clinician handles any clinical message.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
From these staff notes or this approved transcript [paste], draft a short summary: caller, request, who owns the follow-up, and due date. Use this message format [paste]. Label the top "DRAFT, for staff review." Do not add facts that are not in the notes. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, billing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include any patient names or health information.
