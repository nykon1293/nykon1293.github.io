The problem

Owners start the day reacting to whatever is on top of the inbox. Prep for meetings happens in the car. Overdue items surface late.

What SI does

SI reads today's calendar, recent email, the task list, and the CRM. It writes a short briefing. It lists meetings with context on each attendee. It lists messages that need a reply today. It lists tasks overdue or due today. It lists deals or projects with no activity for a while. It is sent at a set time by email or chat.

Related in this library: Weekly money-in digest of payments and what is owed.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Owner of an HVAC company

    The briefing lists two commercial bids due this week. It lists a call with a supplier rep. It lists three customer complaints from yesterday.

  • Executive director of a nonprofit

    Today's donor meeting comes with the donor's giving history and last conversation notes.

  • Financial advisor

    Client meetings come with notes from the last review. The list shows clients who emailed with questions.

What it takes to set up

Read access to calendar, email, tasks, and CRM. Setup is often a day or two.

What a human still checks

  • The briefing is read-only. Any follow-up action is approved.
  • Confidential threads can be excluded.
  • Content is reviewed after the first week. It is trimmed to what is useful.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Every morning at [time], read my calendar, email from the last [24 hours], tasks and [CRM]. Write a short briefing: today's meetings with context, messages needing a reply today, overdue tasks, and deals or projects with no activity in [14] days. Send it to [email/chat]. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

The day starts with a plan. Meetings start better prepared.
