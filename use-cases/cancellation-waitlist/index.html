The problem

A cancellation leaves a gap. Patients who wanted an earlier slot sit on a list nobody has time to work.

What SI does

SI keeps the waitlist organized. It tracks who, what appointment type, preferred times, and how they asked to be contacted. When a slot opens, it drafts an offer to the next eligible people per the practice's own rules. It prepares the order to contact. An event or a class can use the same waitlist when a seat opens. A person still offers the seat.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Two-dentist practice

    A morning cancellation is matched to the next person on the list who asked for a morning visit. Staff review the draft offer, which has no clinical detail, and place the call.

  • Independent chiropractic clinic

    The waitlist is grouped by appointment type and preferred time. When [Appointment time] opens, staff get a draft in the order the clinic already set.

  • Specialty clinic

    An opening is offered only to people the practice marked as eligible for that visit type. Staff decide whether to book it.

  • Cooking studio

    A Saturday tasting drops a guest. SI drafts an offer to the next names on that class waitlist, with the time and what is included from your notes.

  • U-pick farm

    A Sunday morning slot opens. The draft goes to the next household that asked for Sunday, in the order the farm already wrote down.

  • Holiday workshop shop

    A wreath-making evening has a cancellation. SI drafts a short offer with the remaining seats you named. Payment is left for the owner to take.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the practice connects anything that holds patient information, it confirms the tool is covered by a signed BAA. It sets role-based access. It limits SI to the minimum necessary. Start with sample or de-identified material. Start from the practice's waitlist export. Use the written order for who is offered a slot. Permission is to read and draft, not to book or send.

What a human still checks

  • A staff member or clinician approves before anything goes to a patient, a payer, or any outside party.
  • SI never sends, submits, bills, or posts on its own.
  • Anything unusual goes to a "needs clinician or manager review" queue.
  • The practice decides what patient data SI may see. Testing starts with synthetic or de-identified samples.
  • Staff confirm who is offered a slot and in what order. Staff book it. A clinician decides which patients are appropriate for which slots.
  • A person confirms who is offered a seat and sends the message.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Here is the waitlist [paste] and the rules for who is offered a slot [paste]. A slot opened at [Appointment time] for [appointment type]. Draft an offer, with no clinical detail, to the next eligible people in the order those rules set. Do not decide who is urgent and do not book or send anything. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, billing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include any patient names or health information.
