The problem

A shared inbox mixes customer questions, vendor mail, invoices, newsletters, and spam. Urgent messages get buried. The first hour of the day goes to figuring out what needs attention. That time should go to answering it.

What SI does

SI reads new messages. It applies labels you define. Examples: new request, existing customer, billing, vendor, needs owner, ignore. It pulls out the key detail for each. That can be a job address, an account name, or a requested date. It marks anything time-sensitive. It writes a short summary at the top of each queue. It leaves draft replies for routine questions. Nothing goes out without a person pressing send.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Plumbing and heating contractor

    Overnight emails are split into emergency calls, such as no heat or an active leak. New estimate requests have the address and problem pulled out. Supplier mail is in its own pile. The dispatcher starts with the emergencies.

  • Accounting practice

    During busy season, client emails are labeled by client and topic. Topics include a missing document, a question about a return, and payment. Anything that mentions a notice or deadline is flagged for a partner.

  • Property management office

    Tenant emails are sorted into maintenance, rent, and lease questions. Prospective-tenant inquiries go in their own queue. Maintenance messages are marked by urgency.

What it takes to set up

Connect the inbox with permission to read, label, and draft, but not send. Write down your queue names. Write what counts as urgent. Add a handful of good past replies to match your tone. Setup is often a few days. Then you spend a week or two adjusting the rules.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Go through the unread messages in [inbox] from the last [24 hours]. Label each one as [list of queues]. Mark anything that mentions [urgent terms, e.g. leak, deadline, cancel] as urgent. For each message, write a one-line summary and pull out [key details, e.g. address, account, date]. Draft replies only for messages that match these examples: [paste 3-5 good past replies]. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

The day starts with a sorted list instead of a raw inbox. Urgent messages are less likely to sit unseen.
