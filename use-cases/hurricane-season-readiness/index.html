Hurricane-season readiness and continuity plan

Before a storm, SI (Super Intelligence, the new name for AI) turns your own records into a checklist. It shows what to protect, who to notify, and which files to copy. You act on it.

Worth knowing: This is a business checklist from your files. It is not an emergency-management plan and not a client result. Safety decisions stay with you.

The problem

Hurricane season arrives and the plan is still in someone's head. Inventory location, customer appointments, and supplier contacts are scattered. So are the files the business would need after a closure. Nothing else in the library is an emergency or keep-going checklist.

What SI does

From the lists and notes you provide, SI drafts a readiness packet. It shows where stock or equipment sits. It shows which appointments or orders fall in a date range you name. It drafts a customer note you can send if you close. It lists supplier and staff contacts. It lists records to copy, such as orders, invoices, contacts, and insurance documents you already have. It marks gaps, such as a missing backup location or no after-hours contact. It does not send the customer note, move inventory, or file an insurance claim.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Retail shop

    SI lists high-value inventory locations from the stock sheet. It drafts a closure note for customers with upcoming pickups. It lists the order export to copy off site.

  • Home-services company

    The week's appointments are listed and a reschedule note is drafted. The crew contact list is checked for missing phone numbers.

  • Restaurant

    Perishable inventory from the last vendor orders is listed for a decision. A short closure post and email are drafted for the owner to approve.

What it takes to set up

You need your current contacts and a calendar or order export. Add a short note on where inventory and key files live. Add any written closure policy you already use. Setup is often a day or two, then a refresh when a storm is in the forecast.

What a human still checks

  • The owner decides what is actually done and what is sent.
  • Customer and staff messages wait for approval.
  • Insurance and legal steps stay with the owner and their agent or attorney. SI only lists documents you already have.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Using these files [contacts, calendar or orders, inventory notes, closure policy], draft a hurricane readiness checklist for [dates]. Include where stock or equipment is, appointments or orders in that window, a customer message if we close, staff and supplier contacts, and which records to copy. List anything missing. Do not send messages or change records. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting, or changing anything.

Typical effect

The owner has one checklist from the business's own records. Then they decide what to move, cancel, or tell customers.
