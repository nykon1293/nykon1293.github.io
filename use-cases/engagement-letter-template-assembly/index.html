Engagement-letter template assembly for attorney review

SI (Super Intelligence, the new name for AI) fills the firm's approved engagement-letter template from intake data. It highlights every field it filled. It lists blanks or mismatches.

Office help for solo attorneys and small firms. SI drafts and organizes. Attorneys review and decide. No legal advice. No filing-ready work. Client confidentiality comes first.

Worth knowing: This is office support only. It is not legal advice. It does not replace an attorney's judgment. An attorney reviews and approves anything that faces a client or is substantive. Client-confidential information is used only with the firm's agreements, access controls, and consent. SI assembles from the firm's approved language. SI never writes new legal terms. SI never writes new fee terms. SI never writes new scope-of-representation language. The output is a draft, not a final document.

The problem

Staff often copy an old engagement letter and edit it. That leaves stale names, wrong fee terms, or missing clauses.

What SI does

SI fills the firm's attorney-approved template from the intake data. That includes names, the matter description, and the fee arrangement the attorney already selected. SI highlights every field it filled. It lists any blanks or mismatches. One example is a name spelled two ways.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Solo family-law practice

    The template is filled with the names and the fee arrangement the attorney already selected. A list shows every field SI filled.

  • Two-attorney estate-planning firm

    The draft flags a blank where a second name was missing from the intake. It also flags a name that appears in two spellings.

  • Small commercial-litigation firm

    The matter description from intake is placed only in the template's description field. The letter stays a draft until the attorney edits it.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the firm connects anything that holds client information, it reviews the vendor's terms. It checks data retention and training practices. It confirms access controls. It gets any required client consent. Start with sample or de-identified material. Start from the firm's attorney-approved template. Use a de-identified intake that includes the fields the template expects. Setup is often a few days. Most of that time is spent marking which fields SI may fill.

What a human still checks

  • The attorney reviews and edits the full letter. The attorney chooses the terms and sends it.
  • The client signs the letter the attorney sent.
  • SI never sends, files, or posts on its own.
  • The firm decides what client data SI may see. Testing starts with non-confidential or de-identified samples.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Fill this attorney-approved engagement-letter template [paste] using only these intake fields [paste sample or de-identified data], including the fee arrangement the attorney already selected: [paste]. Highlight every field you filled. List blanks and any name spelled more than one way. Do not write new terms, fee language, or scope language. Pause and show me before sending, filing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include client-confidential details.

Typical effect

The draft starts from the firm's approved language. Filled fields and blanks are marked for the attorney.
