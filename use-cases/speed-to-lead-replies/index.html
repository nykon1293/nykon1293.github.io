Speed-to-lead replies for new inquiries

When a new inquiry arrives, SI drafts a specific first reply right away and leaves it for a person to send.

The problem

New inquiries sit for hours while the day gets busy, and the person calls the next business. Turning an inquiry into a CRM record keeps the lead. It does not answer them.

What SI does

SI watches the form, inbox, or voicemail notes you point it at. For a new inquiry it pulls out what they asked, the contact, and any date or address, then drafts a short first reply in your voice: that you got it, the next step, and one question you still need. It does not invent availability, prices, or promises. After-hours inquiries are queued for the morning with the draft ready. A person sends it.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are illustrations, not client results.

  • Contractor

    A form asks for a fence estimate with the city and a photo. SI drafts a reply that confirms the city is in the service area and asks for the missing gate width, for the owner to send.

  • Tutor

    An evening email asks about algebra help. A draft is waiting in the morning with the subjects you listed and a request for the student's grade, and no claim that a specific hour is open.

  • Clinic

    A new-patient form arrives at lunch. SI drafts a reply with what to bring to a first visit from your intake notes, and leaves insurance questions for the front desk.

What it takes to set up

The place inquiries arrive, a few replies you liked, and the facts the draft is allowed to use (hours, service area, what you need from them). A rule for what must go to a person with no draft, such as an emergency or a complaint. Setup is often a few days.

What a human still checks

  • A person sends every first reply.
  • SI does not quote a price or a time that is not in the material you gave it.
  • Emergencies and complaints are flagged for a person instead of getting a routine draft.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool of choice and swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Watch new inquiries in [inbox or form]. For each one, pull out the ask, contact, and any date, address, or other detail. Draft a short first reply using only these facts and this tone [paste examples and allowed facts]. Do not invent prices, open times, or promises. Flag emergencies and complaints for me with no routine draft. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting, or changing anything.

Typical effect

New inquiries get a specific draft while the request is still fresh, and a person still sends it.
