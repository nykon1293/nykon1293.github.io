Office help for small practices. SI drafts, tracks, and organizes. Clinicians and staff review and decide. No medical advice. No clinical decisions. Patient information stays in tools covered by a BAA.

Worth knowing: This is office support only. SI does not name a condition. SI does not recommend treatment. SI does not make clinical decisions. SI does not replace a clinician's judgment. A clinician or trained staff member reviews anything clinical, billing-related, or patient-facing. They must approve it. Patient information (PHI) goes only into tools covered by a signed business associate agreement. Use access controls. Use only the minimum necessary. Never put PHI in consumer tools. SI never decides who needs care. SI never says what a patient should do medically. Messages are generic reminders only. They have no health details. They have no promotions or offers tied to PHI without the authorization the rules require. They make no outcome promises. Texting and recording rules vary. Get consent. Honor opt-outs.

The problem

Patients due for cleanings, check-ups, follow-ups, or re-evaluations slip through. Recall lists are manual.

What SI does

SI builds the recall list from the practice's own recall rules. It drafts friendly, generic reminders to book. It tracks who has responded. It gives staff a short list for personal calls.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Two-dentist practice

    People due under the practice's own cleaning interval are listed. The draft reminder asks them to book and names no procedure.

  • Small family-medicine practice

    A recall list follows the interval the practice set. Staff get a short call list for people who have not replied. Opt-outs are left off.

  • Independent chiropractic clinic

    Generic book-a-visit drafts wait for approval. The message has no health details and no promise about results.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before the practice connects anything that holds patient information, it confirms the tool is covered by a signed BAA. It sets role-based access. It limits SI to the minimum necessary. Start with sample or de-identified material. The practice defines who is due and the interval. Outreach uses approved generic wording with no health details. Honor opt-outs and contact preferences.

What a human still checks

  • A staff member or clinician approves before anything goes to a patient, a payer, or any outside party.
  • SI never sends, submits, bills, or posts on its own.
  • Anything unusual goes to a "needs clinician or manager review" queue.
  • The practice decides what patient data SI may see. Testing starts with synthetic or de-identified samples.
  • The practice approves the wording and sends the outreach. Staff book the visit. Clinicians decide what follow-up is appropriate for any patient.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Using these recall rules [paste] and this list [paste], list who is due. Draft a generic reminder to book from this approved wording [paste], with no health details and no offers. Track who has responded, and make a short call list for staff. Do not send anything. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, billing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include any patient names or health information.

Typical effect

Staff get a recall list and generic draft reminders based on the practice's own intervals. A person still sends them.
