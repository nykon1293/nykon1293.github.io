The problem

Dashboards show that something changed but not why. Someone has to dig through reports to find out. Often nobody does.

What SI does

SI watches a few chosen numbers. When one moves past a threshold you set, it checks the obvious splits. Splits can be by location, product, customer, staff member, channel, or day. It writes a short note. The note says what changed. It says where the change is concentrated. It says what to look at next. It presents causes as possibilities.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Veterinary practice

    New-client visits dropped a lot this month. The note shows the drop is almost all from one referral source. That source stopped sending patients.

  • Restaurant group

    Food cost rose at one location. The note points to a price increase on one supplier's invoices. It also points to a menu item selling more than usual.

  • Nonprofit

    Monthly online donations fell. The note shows recurring donors whose cards expired.

What it takes to set up

Read access to the source data. Thresholds for each number. Setup is often several days.

What a human still checks

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Watch these measures [list] in [data source]. When one changes by more than [threshold] versus [comparison period], break it down by [dimensions] and write a short note on where the change is concentrated and what to check next. Present causes as possibilities. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

Changes get explained sooner. The owner spends less time digging through reports.
