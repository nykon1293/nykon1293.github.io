Legal practices

Document organization and consistent file naming

SI proposes a folder pattern and a rename list for matter files, and waits for a person to approve it before anything moves.

Admin and operations help for solo attorneys and small firms. SI drafts and organizes; attorneys review and decide. No legal advice, no filing-ready work product, and client confidentiality comes first.

Worth knowing: Administrative support only: this is not legal advice and does not replace attorney judgment. An attorney reviews and approves anything client-facing or substantive, and client-confidential information is used only with the firm's agreements, access controls and consent. This is organization only. SI does not decide what is privileged, relevant, or responsive, does not delete or alter documents, and works only inside systems the firm has approved and secured.

The problem

Matter folders fill with names like scan0043.pdf and final_v2_REAL.docx, so files are hard to find and hard to hand off.

What SI does

SI proposes a standard folder structure and naming convention, then suggests renames and filing locations for each document using date, type, and party. It produces a change list before moving anything, and an index of the folder.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are illustrations, not client results.

  • Small commercial-litigation firm

    A change list proposes names with date, document type, and party, and nothing is renamed until a person approves the list.

  • Solo family-law practice

    A folder index groups scans by type so a handoff does not depend on remembering which file is which.

  • Two-attorney estate-planning firm

    The proposed folders follow the firm's own matter template, and the attorney sets any confidentiality labels.

What it takes to set up

GemmiSI sets this up on your own equipment and accounts; your data stays with you. Before connecting anything that holds client information, the firm reviews the vendor's terms, data retention and training practices, confirms access controls, and obtains any required client consent. Start with sample or de-identified material. Use a sample matter folder the firm is comfortable testing, and the naming pattern the firm wants. Work only inside systems the firm has already approved. Setup is often a few days.

What a human still checks

  • A staff member or attorney approves the naming scheme and the change list before anything is renamed or moved.
  • The attorney sets privilege and confidentiality designations.
  • SI never sends, files, or posts on its own.
  • The firm decides what client data SI may see, and starts with non-confidential or de-identified samples when testing.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool of choice and swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Look at this sample file list [paste names only, or a de-identified folder]. Propose a folder structure and a naming pattern using date, document type, and party. Produce a change list and an index. Do not rename, move, delete, or alter any file. Pause and show me before sending, filing, posting or changing anything. Use only the sample or de-identified information I provide; do not include client-confidential details.

Typical effect

A change list and folder index are ready for a person to approve before any file is renamed or moved.
