The problem

Requests arrive by phone, text, email, and portal. They get written on paper, entered late, or lost. Customers do not know when someone is coming.

What SI does

SI reads each request. It creates a job ticket with the location, problem, urgency, and any photos. It checks capacity or vendor availability. It proposes a time slot or the right vendor by your rules. It drafts the message to the customer or tenant with the plan. It drafts the dispatch note to the technician or vendor. A contractor can draft a work order from a signed contract and check that insurance is on file. Crew dispatch still waits for the owner.

Related in this library: Vendor and work-order tracking, Missed-call text-back and after-hours sorting.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are examples, not client results.

  • Property management company

    A tenant's email about a leaking kitchen faucet becomes a ticket marked routine. It is assigned to the plumbing vendor. A draft reply gives the expected visit window.

  • Commercial cleaning company

    A client's request for an extra floor-waxing visit is scheduled into the next open night crew slot.

  • School facilities office

    Teacher requests for repairs are logged by building and room. They are grouped into the custodian's weekly plan.

What it takes to set up

Access to the request channels. The job or maintenance system. Your urgency and assignment rules. Setup is often one to two weeks.

What a human still checks

  • A dispatcher or manager approves assignments and customer messages.
  • Emergencies follow your escalation rules. A person confirms them.
  • Vendor work orders above a set amount need approval.
  • The agent, broker, or manager approves before anything goes to a client, tenant, applicant, or vendor.
  • SI never sends, posts, signs, or changes records on its own.
  • Unusual items go to a "needs manager or attorney review" queue.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool. Swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
For each request in [channels], create a ticket with location, problem, urgency per [rules], and attachments. Propose a time or vendor using [capacity calendar or vendor list]. Draft a message to the requester with the plan, and a dispatch note to the assignee. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

Requests are captured and acknowledged in a consistent way. Customers know what to expect.
