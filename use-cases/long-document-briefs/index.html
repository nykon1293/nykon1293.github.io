Short briefs from long documents

SI reads long documents such as regulations, bid packages, reports or board packets and writes a short brief with what matters to you and what to do next.

Worth knowing: When the document is a regulation, contract or legal notice, the brief is not legal advice; confirm interpretations with a qualified professional.

The problem

Important documents are long and dense, and reading them carefully takes time nobody has. Key requirements and deadlines get missed.

What SI does

SI reads the document and writes a brief: what it is, the key points, requirements or decisions that affect you, deadlines, and open questions, with page references for each point so you can check the source.

Examples

The same job looks different in different businesses. These are illustrations, not client results.

  • General contractor

    A large bid package becomes a brief with scope, submission deadline, bonding and insurance requirements and pre-bid meeting date, with page numbers.

  • Restaurant group

    A city's new food-service regulations are summarized with what changes for kitchens and when it takes effect.

  • School board member

    A long board packet becomes a two-page brief with the votes coming up and the questions worth asking.

What it takes to set up

The document and a short description of what you care about. Setup is minimal.

What a human still checks

  • Important points are checked against the page references.
  • Interpretations of rules are confirmed by a qualified person.
  • SI flags uncertainty rather than guessing.

Try it yourself

Paste this into your SI tool of choice and swap the [bracketed] parts for your own details. It asks SI to stop and show you before it does anything.

Copy-ready prompt 
Read this document [file]. I am [role] at [organization] and care about [topics]. Write a brief with key points, requirements affecting us, deadlines and open questions, with page references for each. Pause and show me before sending, submitting, posting or changing anything.

Typical effect

Long documents get a careful first read quickly, and the important parts are easier to find.
